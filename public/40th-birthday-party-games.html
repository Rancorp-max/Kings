40th birthday party games that get everyone talking

What makes a good milestone-birthday game

By forty, a guest list spans every chapter of someone's life: school friends, university roommates, colleagues, neighbours and family. The best party games give each group a moment to shine and a reason to mix. They also celebrate the guest of honour without roasting them into the ground — the aim is a room full of stories, not an embarrassing slideshow.

Game 1: Who knows the birthday star best?

A multiple-choice quiz about the person turning forty. Guests answer on their phones within 20 seconds; faster correct answers score more, and the live leaderboard sparks friendly rivalry between the school friends and the work friends. Good questions include:

  • What was their first job?
  • What did they want to be as a kid?
  • Early bird or night owl?
  • Which decade's music do they love most?
  • What is their best party trick?
  • What would they do with a surprise day off?
  • What is their dream destination?
  • What is their go-to comfort food?

Add a few facts only a handful of guests would know — the year they learned to surf, the band they followed across the country — and the Party Pass writes personalised questions with convincing wrong answers.

Game 2: Stories, guesses and toasts

The card deck mode passes the turn around the room. Each card is a prompt tied to the guest of honour: tell the story of how you met them, list forty things you are grateful for in thirty seconds, guess their favourite year so far, or give a one-line toast to the next decade. The prompts adapt to the age you choose — thirty, forty, fifty, sixty or seventy — so the same game works for every milestone.

Cards come in playful categories: advice, guesses, would-you-rathers, quick-fire rounds, group votes ("point at who has known them longest") and toasts. It is a great way to hear stories that would never come up otherwise.

Game 3: A keepsake of wishes

Throughout the night, guests can leave a note: a piece of advice for the next decade, a wish, or a prediction. At the end, the host downloads a print-ready PDF with a cover page and one card per guest. It makes a thoughtful gift that costs nothing and takes no extra time on the night.

How to set it up

  1. Open PartyDeck, choose Milestone Birthday and enter the name and the age they are turning.
  2. Pick the quiz or the card deck. For the quiz, tick the correct answers or write your own questions.
  3. When guests arrive, show the QR code. They join from their phone's browser — no app, no sign-up.
  4. Put the host screen on a TV, start the game and let it run between courses or before the cake.

Tips for a great night

  • Keep it kind. Our content avoids "over the hill" jokes and comments about appearance. Forty is a celebration, not a punchline.
  • Mix the teams. Everyone plays individually, but the leaderboard naturally sets up rivalries between friend groups.
  • Time it right. A 12-question quiz takes about fifteen minutes. Play it after food, when people are settled but before the dancing starts.
  • Plan for remote friends. Share the host screen on a video call and far-flung friends can play on their phones too.

Frequently asked questions

Does it work for 30th, 50th and 60th birthdays?

Yes. Choose the age during setup and the cards and questions adjust.

How many people can play?

Up to 8 players free, or up to 40 with a one-time Party Pass for the event.

Can the birthday star play too?

In the card deck mode, yes. In the quiz, they usually watch — but they can join if someone else sets the answers.

Is there anything to download?

No app is needed. Guests play in their browser. The only download is the optional keepsake PDF.

Ready to play?

Free for up to 8 players. No app, no sign-up — guests join with a code.
