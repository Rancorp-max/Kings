Why play King's Cup online?

For adults of legal drinking age (19+ in most of Canada). Play responsibly: any sip can be water, and Gentle mode is on by default.

A real deck of cards gets bent, soaked and lost under the sofa. It also relies on one person remembering every rule, who the Question Master is, and which three new rules have been added since the last King. An online version handles all of that. Everyone sees the same card at the same time, the current rule is written on screen, and the game keeps track of mates, the Question Master and every King's rule so nobody can "forget" the one they don't like.

It also works when you are not in the same room. Friends in different cities can join from their phones while you chat on a video call.

How it works

  1. Host a room. Open PartyDeck, choose King's Cup and confirm you're 19 or older.
  2. Share the code. Friends scan the QR code or type the four-letter code, then choose an avatar, a colour and a name. No app, no account.
  3. Take turns. The screen shows whose turn it is. On your turn, the deck glows — tap it to draw. The card and its rule appear on every phone.
  4. Make choices. Drew a 2 or an 8? Pick a player from the list. A Jack? Choose or type a category. A King? Write a new rule for everyone.
  5. Watch the cup fill. The cup meter fills with each King. The fourth King ends the game with a flourish.

Features that make it better than cards

  • House rules panel: every King's rule, the current Question Master and all mates are listed so nobody can argue.
  • Reactions: tap an emoji and it floats across every screen.
  • Turn skipping: if someone's phone dies or they step away, their turn is skipped automatically after 15 seconds.
  • Reconnect: refresh the page or lose signal and you rejoin the same game where you left off.
  • Gentle mode: the host can switch the wording between "sip or penalty" and the classic "drink".
  • Sounds and confetti: your phone chimes when it's your turn, and Kings get a proper celebration.

Tips for a great online game

Four to eight players is the sweet spot. On a video call, have the host share their screen so everyone can see reactions, while each player uses their phone to draw. Agree on house rules before you start — and keep them fun. The best King's rules are silly ones: talk like a pirate, no pointing, compliment the person to your left.

Above all, keep it relaxed. Anyone can sit a round out, and any sip can be water. The game is about the people around the table, not the drinks.

Frequently asked questions

Is it free?

Yes, King's Cup is free for up to 8 players.

Do all players need the app?

There's no app to install — everyone plays in their phone's browser.

Can we play remotely?

Yes. Anyone with the room code can join from anywhere; pair it with a video call.

What if someone loses connection?

They rejoin automatically when their phone reconnects, and their turn is skipped if they're gone for more than 15 seconds.
