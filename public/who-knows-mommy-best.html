"Who knows Mommy best?" — the quiz, made easy

The most-requested baby shower game, without the printouts: guests answer on their phones, the scores add up themselves, and the mom-to-be gets to watch the room compete over how well they know her.

Start a free room →

How the game works

Before the shower, the host collects answers from the mom-to-be — or from her partner, which adds a fun twist. During the party, each question appears on the big screen with four options. Guests pick an answer on their phones within 20 seconds. A correct answer scores 500 points plus a speed bonus of up to 500, and the leaderboard appears after each question. Whoever knows her best takes the top of the podium at the end.

Because scoring is automatic, the mom-to-be does not have to mark anything. She gets to enjoy the reactions — and the arguments about whether her favourite pizza topping is really pineapple.

30 question ideas

Pick 10 to 15. Mix easy questions that everyone can guess with a few deep cuts only her closest friends will know.

Her favourites

  • What is her go-to comfort food?
  • What is her favourite season?
  • Coffee, tea, juice or water first thing in the morning?
  • What is her dream holiday: beach, city, mountains or road trip?
  • What is her favourite way to spend a Sunday?
  • Which dessert could she never turn down?

Her story

  • What was her first job?
  • What did she want to be when she was little?
  • Where did she and her partner meet?
  • What was her favourite subject at school?
  • Which city has she always wanted to visit?
  • What is the bravest thing she has ever done?

Her habits

  • Early bird or night owl?
  • What is her secret talent?
  • Which chore does she actually enjoy?
  • What does she always have in her bag?
  • How many alarms does she set in the morning?
  • What song does she sing in the car?

Becoming a parent

  • Which baby milestone is she most excited about?
  • Which lullaby will she sing first?
  • Who will change the first diaper: her or her partner?
  • What is one family tradition she wants to pass on?
  • What was the first baby item she bought?
  • Which relative will be the baby's favourite babysitter?

Just for fun

  • What would she name a pet goldfish?
  • If she had a superpower, what would it be?
  • What is her karaoke style?
  • Which emoji does she use the most?
  • What is the best gift anyone ever gave her?
  • What is her unpopular food opinion?

Tips for great multiple-choice answers

The wrong options matter as much as the right one. Make them plausible — if the answer is "at a friend's wedding", the other options could be "at work", "through a dating app" and "at university", not something obviously silly. Keep answers short so they fit on a phone screen, and mix up which position the correct answer sits in. Add a "Mom or Dad: who said it?" round

After the main quiz, switch to two-choice questions. Read a quote or a "who is more likely to…" line and guests pick Mom or Dad. It is quick, it gives the partner a moment in the spotlight, and the results are often surprising. Finish with baby predictions if you like — due date, weight and hair colour — scored after the birth.

What you need

  • A host device on a big screen (a laptop connected to a TV works well, but a tablet or phone is fine for small groups).
  • Guests' phones with a data connection.
  • Ten minutes of prep to check the answers.

No printing, no pens, no score sheets — and nothing to clean up afterwards.

Frequently asked questions

Should the mom-to-be play?

She usually hosts or watches — it's her quiz! If she wants to play, have her partner set the answers instead.

How many questions should I use?

Ten to fifteen keeps the energy up. Each question takes about 30 seconds with the reveal and leaderboard.

Can I make it "Who knows Daddy best?" instead?

Yes. Enter any name as the guest of honour and the questions update automatically.

What if a guest arrives late?

They can join mid-quiz with the room code and start scoring from the next question.

Ready to play?

Free for up to 8 players. No app, no sign-up — guests join with a code.
