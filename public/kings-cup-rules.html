King's Cup rules — every card explained

King's Cup (also called Kings, Ring of Fire or Circle of Death) is a classic card game for adults. Here is how each card works, common variations, and how to keep the game fun for everyone.

Before you start

King's Cup is a drinking game for adults of legal drinking age (19+ in most of Canada). Nobody should ever feel pressured to drink. Every rule below works with sips of any drink — water and soda included — or with light penalties instead. Never drink and drive.

Traditionally, players sit around a table with a cup in the middle and cards spread face-down in a circle around it. Players take turns drawing a card and following its rule. The game ends when the fourth King is drawn.

The rules for each card

Ace — Never Have I Ever
Everyone holds up three fingers. Going around the circle, each player says something they have never done. Anyone who has done it lowers a finger. The first player out of fingers takes a sip.
2 — You
The drawer picks any player to take a sip.
3 — Me
The drawer takes a sip.
4 — Floor
Everyone touches the floor. The last person to do so takes a sip.
5 — Guys
All the guys take a sip.
6 — Girls
All the girls take a sip. (Many groups swap 5 and 6 for "anyone wearing jeans" and "anyone wearing glasses" to keep things inclusive.)
7 — Heaven
Everyone points to the sky. Last hand up takes a sip.
8 — Mate
The drawer picks a mate. For the rest of the game, whenever one of them sips, the other does too.
9 — Rhyme
The drawer says a word; going around, each player says a word that rhymes. The first to hesitate or repeat takes a sip.
10 — Waterfall
Everyone starts sipping at the same time. Nobody can stop until the person before them stops.
Jack — Categories
The drawer names a category (dog breeds, pizza toppings…). Going around, each player names something in it. The first to blank takes a sip.
Queen — Question Master
The drawer becomes Question Master until the next Queen. Anyone who answers a question they ask takes a sip.
King — King's Cup
The drawer makes a new rule everyone must follow, and pours a splash into the cup in the middle. Whoever draws the fourth King finishes the cup — in a gentle game, that means a single sip or a silly penalty.

Popular house rules

  • Thumb Master: the player who draws the rule can put a thumb on the table at any time; the last to copy them takes a sip.
  • No names: anyone who uses a first name takes a sip.
  • Breaking the circle: in the physical version, whoever breaks the ring of cards takes a sip.
  • Snake Eyes: anyone who makes eye contact with the snake-eyes player takes a sip until the rule passes on.

New rules from Kings are the heart of the game — keep them silly rather than punishing. "Talk like a pirate" beats "finish your drink" every time.

Playing responsibly

  • Agree before you start that anyone can swap a sip for a sip of water or a small penalty — no questions asked.
  • Replace "finish the cup" with a single sip or a dare.
  • Eat first, keep water on the table and plan everyone's ride home.

Frequently asked questions

How many players do you need?

Three or more works; four to eight is ideal.

Can you play King's Cup without alcohol?

Absolutely. Use any drink, or replace sips with light penalties — the fun is in the rules, not the drinking.

What happens with the fourth King?

The game ends. Traditionally that player finishes the cup; in Gentle mode it's a single sip or a silly dare.

Is King's Cup the same as Ring of Fire?

Yes — Kings, King's Cup, Ring of Fire and Circle of Death are regional names for the same game, with small rule differences.
